      <p class="lead text-lg text-brand-gray-dark font-medium mb-6">
        With India's hot, humid climate and a monsoon season that can stretch for months, a portable dehumidifier has become less of a luxury and more of a practical necessity for many homes and small offices. But with so many options available, choosing the right one can feel confusing. This guide breaks it down into simple terms.
      </p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Do You Actually Need a Dehumidifier?</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Before buying, check for these common signs of excess indoor humidity: a musty smell in closed rooms, condensation on windows, mirrors, or walls, mould spots in bathrooms, cupboards, or corners, clothes or leather goods feeling damp or developing a smell, or a generally sticky, heavy feeling in the air, especially during monsoon. If you're noticing two or more of these, a dehumidifier will likely make a noticeable difference.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Key Factors to Consider When Buying</h3>

      <h4 class="text-lg font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-6 mb-2">Room Size and Capacity</h4>
      <p class="mb-4">Dehumidifiers are rated by how many litres of moisture they can remove per day. As a general guide, small rooms up to 200 square feet usually need a capacity of 10 to 20 litres a day. Medium rooms between 200 and 500 square feet do better with 20 to 30 litres a day. Large rooms or open spaces above 500 square feet typically need 30 litres a day or more. Buying a unit that's too small for the space means it will run constantly without keeping up, while an oversized unit wastes energy and money.</p>

      <h4 class="text-lg font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-6 mb-2">Type of Dehumidifier</h4>
      <p class="mb-4">Compressor based, or refrigerant, dehumidifiers work well in warmer, more humid conditions, which makes them ideal for most of India. They're generally more energy efficient in high humidity environments. Desiccant dehumidifiers use absorbent material to pull moisture from the air. These work well even in cooler temperatures and are often quieter, but can be less efficient in very hot conditions. For most Indian households and offices, a compressor based dehumidifier is the more practical choice.</p>

      <h4 class="text-lg font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-6 mb-2">Tank Capacity and Drainage Options</h4>
      <p class="mb-4">Check how large the water collection tank is and how often it needs emptying. For continuous, low maintenance use, look for models with a continuous drainage hose option, which lets excess water drain out directly without manual emptying.</p>

      <h4 class="text-lg font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-6 mb-2">Noise Level</h4>
      <p class="mb-4">If the dehumidifier will run in a bedroom, office, or living space, check the noise rating, measured in decibels. Look for models designed for quiet operation, especially for overnight use.</p>

      <h4 class="text-lg font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-6 mb-2">Energy Efficiency</h4>
      <p class="mb-4">Since dehumidifiers often run for extended periods, energy consumption adds up. Look for units with a good energy efficiency rating to keep running costs manageable over time.</p>

      <h4 class="text-lg font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-6 mb-2">Additional Features Worth Considering</h4>
      <p class="mb-4">An auto humidistat automatically maintains a set humidity level and switches off once it's reached, saving energy. Auto restart resumes operation automatically after a power cut, which is useful in areas with unstable power supply. A washable air filter makes maintenance easier and improves air quality, and wheels with a compact design make it easier to move the unit between rooms.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Where to Use a Portable Dehumidifier</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Portable dehumidifiers are commonly used in bedrooms and living rooms during monsoon, basements and ground floor rooms prone to dampness, wardrobes and storage rooms to protect clothes and leather goods, small offices with electronic equipment, and bathrooms with poor ventilation.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Final Thoughts</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Choosing the right portable dehumidifier comes down to matching capacity to your room size, picking a type suited to your climate, and considering features that make daily use hassle free. Getting this right means fresher air, less mould, and better protection for your home and belongings all year round.</p>
