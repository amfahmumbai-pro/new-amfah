      <p class="lead text-lg text-brand-gray-dark font-medium mb-6">
        It's just after 8 o'clock in the morning. The doors of the eye hospital open, and the first patients slowly begin to arrive.
      </p>

      <p class="mb-4">An elderly man is waiting for his cataract surgery. A college student has come for a vision test after struggling to read classroom presentations. A young child sits beside her parents, nervous about her very first eye examination.</p>

      <p class="mb-4">Inside the hospital, the day has already started.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Doctors prepare for consultations, nurses organize patient files, and technicians switch on the diagnostic equipment that will be used throughout the day.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Everything feels calm, clean, and well organized.</p>

      <p class="mb-4">But there is one part of the hospital that almost nobody notices &mdash; the air.</p>

      <p class="mb-4">Temperature is easy to feel. Humidity isn't.</p>

      <p class="mb-4">Yet it quietly affects the comfort of patients, the condition of medical equipment, and the overall environment inside the hospital.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">The Rain Outside Doesn't Always Stay Outside</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">During the monsoon season, every hospital faces the same challenge.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Patients enter with umbrellas and raincoats. Doors open hundreds of times throughout the day, allowing humid outdoor air to move inside.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Even when the air conditioning is running, some rooms can still feel slightly damp.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Mirrors may collect moisture in the morning. Windows sometimes develop tiny droplets of water. Storage rooms may lose that fresh feeling after several rainy weeks.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">These small changes happen so slowly that they're easy to ignore.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">But over time, excess humidity begins affecting different parts of the hospital.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Modern Eye Care Depends on Modern Technology</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Years ago, eye examinations relied mainly on basic instruments.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Today, technology plays a much bigger role.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">A patient visiting an eye hospital may undergo several different tests before meeting the doctor.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">The eyes are examined using slit lamps that magnify tiny structures with remarkable clarity.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">OCT scanners produce detailed images of the retina within seconds.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Fundus cameras capture photographs that help doctors monitor eye diseases over time.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Autorefractors quickly measure vision before a detailed examination begins.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">These machines work continuously from morning until evening.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">They represent a significant investment for every hospital.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Providing a stable indoor environment helps support the long-term care of these valuable systems.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Every Surgery Demands a Controlled Environment</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Imagine a patient waiting outside the operation theatre for cataract surgery.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Inside, the medical team is preparing for the procedure.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Every instrument has been sterilized.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">The surgical microscope is positioned carefully.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">The operating lights are adjusted.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Everything is checked before the first incision is made.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Hospitals pay close attention to cleanliness, airflow, and temperature because every detail matters.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Humidity is another part of creating a stable indoor environment.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Balanced humidity helps maintain comfortable conditions for surgeons, nurses, and support staff while supporting the overall environment inside the operation theatre.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Comfort Matters More Than Most People Realize</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Patients visiting an eye hospital are often anxious.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Some worry about losing their vision.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Others are preparing for surgery they've never experienced before.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">The environment around them plays an important role in helping them feel comfortable.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">A fresh waiting area. Clean examination rooms. Comfortable consultation cabins.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">These small details build confidence before the doctor even begins the examination.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">When humidity remains too high, indoor spaces can feel sticky and uncomfortable, especially during long monsoon seasons.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Maintaining balanced humidity helps hospitals create a cleaner and more welcoming atmosphere for everyone who walks through the door.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">The Hospital Keeps Working After the Last Patient Leaves</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">When visiting hours end, the work doesn't stop.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Technicians clean and prepare diagnostic equipment for the next day. Medical supplies are restocked. Operation theatres are cleaned. Patient records are updated. Backup equipment is returned to storage.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">These behind-the-scenes activities are just as important as patient care.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Storage rooms often contain expensive ophthalmic equipment, packaged medical supplies, disposable consumables, and important hospital documents.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Keeping these areas dry helps maintain better storage conditions throughout the year.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Humidity Can Affect More Than One Department</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">People often think humidity is only a problem inside operation theatres.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">In reality, it can influence many different areas of an eye hospital.</p>
      <ul class="list-disc pl-6 my-6 space-y-2.5">
        <li class="leading-relaxed text-brand-gray-dark/90"><strong>Reception Area:</strong> Welcomes hundreds of visitors every day and should always feel fresh and comfortable.</li>
        <li class="leading-relaxed text-brand-gray-dark/90"><strong>Consultation Rooms:</strong> Benefit from a pleasant indoor environment where doctors spend long hours examining patients.</li>
        <li class="leading-relaxed text-brand-gray-dark/90"><strong>Diagnostic Rooms:</strong> Contain sophisticated imaging equipment that operates throughout the day.</li>
        <li class="leading-relaxed text-brand-gray-dark/90"><strong>Storage Areas:</strong> Hold valuable medicines, consumables, and packaged supplies that need organized storage conditions.</li>
        <li class="leading-relaxed text-brand-gray-dark/90"><strong>Administrative Offices & Record Rooms:</strong> Benefit from maintaining balanced humidity, helping paper records remain in good condition for longer.</li>
      </ul>
      <p class="mb-4">Humidity control supports the entire hospital &mdash; not just one department.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Isn't Air Conditioning Enough?</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">This is one of the most common questions hospital owners ask.</p>
      <p class="mb-4"><em>"If every room already has air conditioning, why would we need a dehumidifier?"</em></p>
      <p class="mb-4">The answer is simple.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">An air conditioner is mainly designed to reduce temperature.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">A dehumidifier is designed to reduce excess moisture in the air.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">During humid weather, especially in coastal cities and throughout the monsoon season, indoor humidity can remain high even when the rooms feel cool.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">That's why many hospitals use dedicated humidity control systems alongside their existing air conditioning.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Both systems work together, but each has a different purpose.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">A Small Change That Makes a Big Difference</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Humidity is invisible. Patients don't ask about it. Doctors rarely talk about it.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Yet controlling it helps create a hospital that feels cleaner, fresher, and more comfortable every single day.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">It also supports better care of valuable medical equipment and helps maintain organized storage conditions across different departments.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Sometimes the biggest improvements come from things people never notice.</p>

      <h3 class="text-xl font-bold font-display text-brand-navy mt-8 mb-4">Creating a Better Environment for Patients and Staff</h3>
      <p class="mb-4">Every eye hospital works hard to provide excellent patient care.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Doctors bring years of experience. Modern technology supports accurate diagnosis. Dedicated healthcare professionals keep everything running smoothly.</p>
      <p class="mb-4">Creating the right indoor environment is another part of that commitment.</p>
